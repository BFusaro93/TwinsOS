1. Estimates built on a budget, not a guessEvery line shows its hours, cost, price, and margin as you build it

  • Each service is set to budget one of two ways: Manual (you enter the hours) or Production Rate (hours come from the measured area divided by the service's square feet per man-hour). Hourly lines use the quantity as the hours.
  • Cost fills in from budgeted hours times your fully burdened break-even labor rate, which already includes overhead. Type your own cost on any line to override it. A built-in calculator builds hours from crew size and time.
  • Overhead is either one flat percentage or set by cost type: labor, subcontract, materials, equipment, and other. The summary panel nets it against gross profit to show net profit.
  • Margin is visible on every line, color-coded: green at 30% or more, gray from 10% to 30%, red below 10%. An amber warning flags likely mistakes, such as costs above price or an hourly service priced under your break-even rate.
  • Per-line complexity from 50% to 200% scales price and cost together, and returns exactly to where it started when you dial it back. Fixed-total or per-unit pricing, multiple visits, line and estimate discounts, and tax are built in.
  • No costs entered yet? The summary asks you to set your break-even rate rather than showing a misleading 100% margin.

2. Faster, more consistent pricingMeasurements, rate tables, bundles, and packages do the typing

Pricing tools
  • Rate Matrix: price by bands of a measurement, such as turf square feet, with an overflow rule for larger properties. Copy a matrix to other services.
  • Zone measurements from the client's property, filled in by tracing the lawn and beds on a satellite map.
  • Service bundles: save a set of lines, with products and sub-services, and drop it onto any estimate.
Quote it the way clients buy it
  • Packages: quote a program like a multi-step fertilization plan as one line the client accepts as a whole.
  • Good / Better / Best options with a clear total for each.
  • Equipment costs count against margin but never show on the proposal, PDF, or invoice.
  • An AI Estimate Builder turns a sales-visit transcript into draft line-item wording you review and paste in.

3. Proposals clients can accept onlineOne secure link: review, sign, and pay a deposit

  • Email the estimate or copy its proposal link. Clients review the lines, accept with a typed and drawn signature, decline, or request changes. They can download a PDF copy.
  • The client always sees the last version you sent, so you can keep editing without them seeing a half-finished revision. Set a Valid until date; you get a reminder about three days before it ends.
  • An approval step can hold back an estimate that needs sign-off before it goes out, is accepted, or is converted.
  • Acceptance is permanent on the record: signature, time, and the exact version signed are locked, and each action is written to the estimate's history.
  • Pipeline view and list filters track stages from Draft to Invoiced. Record won and lost reasons to learn from every close.

4. Deposits at acceptanceTake money while the client is ready to commit

  • Set a Deposit Required on the estimate and the client is asked for it as they accept. Options: pay by card or bank transfer (both through Stripe, once your organization is connected) or Skip for now. A deposit never blocks acceptance, and the amount can't be edited by the client.
  • If the client skips it, you and the sales rep are notified and the deposit stays due on their proposal page and portal until it's paid, waived, or the work is paid in full.
  • A card deposit is reserved for that estimate and applied automatically to its invoice when issued. If a deposit fails, the proposal stays accepted, you are notified, and the client is emailed a link to try again.

5. From accepted estimate to workNo re-keying; the price the client agreed to carries through

  • One click on Convert to Job. Choose which lines to include, then pick One Time, Recurring, Project, or Waiting List. Estimates with a package convert to package jobs.
  • Discounts carry over: the job is priced at what the client actually agreed to pay. Crew size, crew, and schedule are set at conversion.
  • Photos and attached files are copied to the job so the crew sees the site as the estimator did. Sub-items copy over as products to bring and tasks to do.
  • Projects can bill by milestones, and you can invoice straight from an estimate. Lines already on a job are locked so the two never drift apart.

6. Job costing: quoted vs. actualThe estimate becomes the budget every job is measured against

MeasureWhat you see
Labor hoursActual man-hours from worked visits, net of breaks, against the estimate's budgeted hours.
Labor costThe crew's real clock-in and clock-out cost. Visits without punches are costed at hours times the crew's average rate, and labeled as such.
MaterialsProducts crews recorded as used, plus materials added on the job, compared with the estimated cost including equipment and sub-items.
Revenue and profitPre-tax revenue, so sales tax never inflates profit.
  • The Job Costing report shows material and labor cost of each completed visit with budget vs. actual detail. A Cost of Goods Sold report and a Service Profitability summary come from the same data.
  • Production Rate Accuracy compares each service's assumed square feet per man-hour with what crews actually achieved, flagging rates to recalibrate. Quote tighter every season.
  • Projects: budget vs. actual man hours and labor cost, original and revised contract price, change orders, and amount remaining to bill.

7. Services & pricing, and bulk price increasesChange prices once, with a preview and an undo

  • Your service catalog holds default rates, rate matrices, and default equipment. Changing a catalog rate never changes work already sold, so each client keeps the price they agreed to.
  • Bulk Prices opens an editable grid of every service. Adjust by percent or flat dollar amount, round to the cent, $0.25, $1, or $5, and include rate-matrix tiers. Nothing saves until you click Save.
  • Price Adjustment runs re-price existing client work: job service rates, package amounts, and package service rates. Narrow by service or job type, preview every line before and after, untick any customer to leave out, then name and apply the run.
  • Undo from Adjustment History. Lines you have since re-priced by hand are left alone. Runs never touch signed contracts, issued invoices, or one-off per-visit overrides.
Control and privacy: bulk price changes and price runs are separate permissions that are off for non-admin roles by default, and the price-run check is enforced in the database itself. Crew logins can't see your rates, costs, or the service catalog. Labor cost on reports is shown only to roles with a job-costing or payroll-cost permission.
