2. Application rates and mix recipesSet up once per product; used everywhere

  • Rate: amount applied per area, such as 1.5 fl oz per 1,000 sq ft or per acre, with an application method (broadcast, backpack sprayer, truck tank) and product cost. Perimeter and barrier products can be rated per linear foot.
  • Mix recipe: mark a rate as Not Mixed, Mixed with Water (e.g. 2 oz chemical per 1 gal water), or Mixed with Products (tank mixes with a second catalog product).
  • Unit-aware math: volume, weight, and area units convert correctly. When a recipe is ambiguous or units can't be safely converted (for example, a linear-foot rate against a square-foot field), the system declines to guess rather than print a wrong dose.
  • Handles both label conventions: rates stated as concentrate and rates stated as finished spray volume.
  • The rate used is kept on the record (e.g. "2 fl oz per 1,000 sq ft"), so reports and notices still show it after the product's rate changes.

3. Services that know their chemicalsChemical programs without re-keying

  • Flag a service (e.g. 7-Step Fertilizer, Grub Control) as chemical-tracking and attach its default products, optionally with start and end dates for the program season.
  • Those defaults pre-populate every dispatched visit's application record automatically, as a planned amount the crew later confirms.
  • Optional auto-calculated quantity: pulls the treatable area and applies the product's rate to compute how much to apply and how much finished mix to prepare. The area comes from, in order:
    1. a treated area on the job service, for a spot treatment or just the front lawn;
    2. the service's own area measurement (e.g. Hardscape Sq Ft for parking-lot weed control);
    3. your company's default area field (e.g. Turf Sq Ft).
    Measurements are read from the property first, then the client. The load list, reports, crew card, and application panel all use the same order, so they always agree.

5. In the field: the crew's mix sheet and confirmationThe right dose, without a calculator, and the record made on the spot

On the crew app (iOS, Android, and browser), each stop shows a Chemical Mix card: the product, how much active chemical to use, and the total finished-mix volume to prepare. Printed chemical route sheets give the same numbers on paper.

  • Confirm what was applied: enter the amount used or tick Not used. The foreman is recorded as applicator, with their license, and a matching mix volume is calculated.
  • How applied and conditions: adjust method, target, and area treated, and record temperature and wind (auto-filled from the nearest weather reading).
  • Add a chemical the office didn't plan, such as a spot treatment or substitute, even on a stop with nothing planned.
  • View label and SDS: EPA #, re-entry interval, restricted-use status, label directions, and the filed Safety Data Sheet, one tap away.
  • Load check-off: crews tick off equipment and materials as they load; the Crew Board shows what's on each truck.
  • Confirmed records are final for the crew. Only the office, with the right permission, can correct one, and the change is audited.

6. Notice of treatment, in every form clients expectDocumentation clients and regulators expect

  • Notice of Treatment built from the application record: date, time, weather, applicator and license #, and each product with EPA #, amount, method, and target. Print it, or email it with a PDF copy attached, after a live preview.
  • Bulk send: select a day's visits and send each client their own notice. Visits are sorted into ready, already sent, no email, bounced, and not yet confirmed, so a second run never double-sends.
  • Automatic for contract clients: an automation can send the notice after a completed visit, so clients billed under a contract (who never get an invoice) still receive the record.
  • Three kinds of chemical email: Client Instruction, Recommendation, and Upsale, each with its own templates built in Documents with merge tags (client, applicator and license #, products applied with EPA # and amount, conditions, care instructions).
  • On the invoice: optionally add a Chemical Application Record page to the invoice PDF (product, EPA #, ratio, areas, amount, method, targets, re-entry, applicator).
  • Per-product "client route sheet instructions" flow into the care instructions automatically.

8. Inventory and purchasing connectionChemicals are products, so stock and buying are part of the same system

  • Chemicals are stocked and purchased through the same product catalog and PO workflow, with fractional quantity on hand (e.g. 2.75 gallons).
  • Stock depletes automatically: when a visit completes, each confirmed chemical comes out of on-hand quantity, converted to the product's unit. Correcting, un-confirming, or reopening puts it back, and tiny remainders carry forward so small applications still add up.
  • Materials Needed compares upcoming chemical demand to on-hand stock and open orders, converting units, so you buy what you actually need.
  • Each rate carries a product cost, and receiving goods against a PO updates stock, so the same catalog drives costing, buying, and application records.
